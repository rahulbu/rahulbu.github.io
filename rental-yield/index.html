How it is worked out
  • Gross yield is a year’s full rent divided by the price. Net yield is the rent actually received (after empty months and agent fees) minus maintenance, property tax and repairs, divided by everything you paid: price, stamp duty and registration, interiors and other one-off costs. It is before income tax and ignores the loan.
  • Rent is taxed at your rate on 70% of the rent received less property tax (the 30% standard deduction). With a loan, the year’s interest is deducted too. Under the new tax regime a loss on a let-out home can’t be set off against your salary, so a loss counts as zero tax in that year and isn’t carried forward.
  • The loan is on the price only; stamp duty, interiors and one-off costs are paid in cash. EMIs are monthly at a fixed rate.
  • Rent rises at the rent rate each year; maintenance, property tax and repairs rise at the cost rate.
  • On sale, selling costs come off the price, and 12.5% tax is charged on the gain over price plus stamp duty and one-off costs (no indexation). Interiors are treated as worn out and worth nothing.
  • The fund grows at the return you set, with 12.5% tax on its gain when cashed out if the sale-tax switch is on. Debt funds and FDs are taxed at your slab instead, so use a lower return for those.
  • “Return” is the internal rate of return over the holding period, counting every year’s cash flow and the sale.
